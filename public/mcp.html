MCP questions, answered honestly.

What is MCP, in plain terms?

MCP — the Model Context Protocol — is an open standard that lets AI assistants work with your existing apps: read from them, act in them, through a connector you control. Your systems stay exactly where they are; the AI gets a safe, permissioned door in. Think of it as a universal adapter between AI assistants and business software.

Which AI assistants work with an MCP connector?

Any assistant that supports MCP — and that list is broad. The standard originated at Anthropic (the makers of Claude) in late 2024, and has since been adopted across the industry: OpenAI, Google and others announced support in 2025. A connector we build for you isn't tied to one vendor's AI.

What if our software has no API?

That's often exactly where a connector helps most. For desktop and legacy software we can connect at the application level — the AI operates the app itself. We've published working proof: Swing MCP, our open-source connector that lets AI assistants operate Java Swing desktop applications — clicking, typing, filling forms, reading tables and dialogs.

How long does an MCP connector take to build?

A first working connector typically takes two to four weeks. The free audit comes first and takes days, not weeks — a call, then a written map of what's worth connecting and in what order.

What does it cost?

The audit is free. After it, the connector is scoped and priced — fixed-price where the shape is clear, short iterations where it isn't — and you approve the scope before anything is billed. If a connector isn't worth it for your situation, the audit will say exactly that.

Who maintains the connector after launch?

Your choice. You own the code either way — documented, running in your own accounts. We're glad to stay on for run & improve: monitoring, updates, security patches and new capabilities as needs grow. And if you'd rather take it in-house, that's fine too.
